## What's new in v0.10.0 (Windows and Linux with an NVIDIA GPU)

### Highlights

- **Music on Windows and Linux with an NVIDIA GPU.** ACE-Step 1.5 turbo and XL turbo write songs with lyrics and instrumentals from 10 to 600 seconds. On an RTX 4090 a 3-minute song takes about 3 seconds (turbo) or 4 seconds (XL) once the model is loaded; the 10-minute maximum takes 11 to 16 seconds. Turbo needs about 8 GB of video memory, XL 12 to 14 GB. Install the music pack from Music Models (about 6.6 GB, in its own folder). Linux, cards below 24 GB and other NVIDIA generations are untested.
- **Stable Audio 3** (sound effects and short music) joins Stable Audio Open on the same machines. Small SFX, Small Music and Medium are gated on Hugging Face: accept Stability's licence on each model page and add your token in Settings.
- **Speech-to-text on the GPU.** A new "More speech-to-text on the NVIDIA GPU" add-on (Voice > Models > Add-ons) adds Qwen3-ASR (1.7B and 0.6B), Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3, Nemotron 3.5 ASR and Granite Speech 5.0. On a 9-second clip the warm time is 0.15 to 0.2 seconds for Parakeet and 1 to 2.6 seconds for the others, and each read English and French back word for word apart from number formatting. A forced aligner gives word timings and speaker labels to the models that return none. The 30B Nemotron Transcribe row is listed but was not measured.
- **Natural voices on the GPU.** The speech add-on now carries Chatterbox (Turbo and Multilingual V3), VoxCPM2 and Orpheus, with voice design and cloning for the first three. Speed on an RTX 4090 is 5.7 seconds per 10 seconds of speech for Chatterbox Turbo, 12 for V3, 19 for VoxCPM2 (about twice real time, and 11 GB of system memory) and 7.8 for Orpheus. Cloned speech is watermarked as before. Nobody has listened to these yet; word-for-word read-back by a speech-to-text model is the only quality check so far.
- **New image and video models** (diffusers and stable-diffusion.cpp on CUDA): FLUX 3 Image Klein (7 s for 1024 px at 8 steps, plus a faster stable-diffusion.cpp Q8 row at 5.5 s), Qwen-Image-2.1 Turbo as a Q4 row (7.5 s), MOSS-Image SFT (53 s at 50 steps, 22 GB of video memory) and Kandinsky 6.0 Lite distilled video with a sound track (110 s for a 5-second clip at 864 by 480). Image editing with FLUX 3 Klein stays Mac-only for now. Krea 2 Turbo is not offered (gated, access not granted).
- **Lip sync (experimental, in progress).** Give Video Studio a vocal track and a start image and LTX-2.3 makes a clip where the mouth follows the audio, on an NVIDIA card with about 22 GB free. A 5-second 768 by 512 shot takes about 70 seconds warm (157 seconds the first time). Sync is better than chance in our measurement (correlation 0.27 to 0.50 against 0.16 to 0.40 for chance) but a person has not judged it. Each shot can make several takes (3 for a sung vocal, 1 for speech, 4 for best quality) and keep the best by a sync score; the score comes from an optional add-on ("Install sync scoring" in the lip-sync panel, about 90 MB, installed on demand), and without it take 1 is used. A real 5.04-second clip with a spoken line took 101.6 seconds, and a two-take shot kept the better-scoring take; on a 4-second sung ballad window none of three takes reached the usable bar (scores 0.38, 0.30, 0.29). Singing works only partly, and the Apple Silicon path has never run. Treat it as a preview.
- **Chat models.** New GGUF rows with measured 4090 speeds: Gemma 4 12B QAT (93 tok/s, 115 to 120 with its drafter), Gemma 4 26B-A4B QAT (160 tok/s, about 175 with its drafter), Nemotron 3.5 Lightning Q4_0 (232 tok/s) and Qwen3.6 35B-A3B UD-IQ4_XS with MTP heads (170 tok/s, no real gain from its heads). Speculative decoding stays off by default. Larger quants of the same families are listed with their sizes and the fit check; they were not run.
- **Nunchaku (SVDQuant INT4) FLUX.1** on NVIDIA: FLUX.1 schnell at 1024 px in about 6 seconds warm. Nunchaku is not on PyPI under that name, so you install its wheel yourself. The Dev INT4 pack carries the FLUX.1 \[dev] non-commercial licence.
- **vLLM in WSL2.** "Install vLLM in WSL" in Settings > Accelerators installs vLLM inside your Ubuntu distro, and the app talks to it as an engine. A 0.6B model answers at about 80 tokens per second through the bridge and gives its memory back on unload. It needs WSL2 and an NVIDIA driver, and the installer names the Ubuntu packages it needs. Only a 24 GB card and one small model were tried.
- **TensorFold 1.x on NVIDIA (CUDA text engine).** A native engine with its own speculative drafter. On Linux x86_64 and ARM64 Setup downloads the release archive and checks its sha256; **Windows has no release archive**, so there you supply your own source build or a self-hosted archive. Measured through the app on the RTX 4090 with Qwen3.8-27B and its DFlash 2 drafter (an older 1.2.x build): 49.6 tokens per second plain, 132 drafted on prose, 206 drafted on code. The 1.3.4 pin was set from release notes and source, without a Windows binary run.
- **Windows CUDA llama.cpp and stable-diffusion.cpp.** Settings > Accelerators installs the CUDA llama-server (b12651, both archives sha256-checked on install; Qwen3.6-35B-A3B Q4_K_M answered at 41.6 tokens per second on the 4090), and stable-diffusion.cpp builds with CUDA on Windows; an installer built with that `sd.exe` carries NVIDIA's CUDA runtime DLLs for it under NVIDIA's licence (see THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md). Release CI does not build `sd`, so a CI installer has no `sd.exe`. Measured with the pinned `532d2c1` CUDA 13 build (FU-399, 2026-11-23): runtime archive 575 MiB, NSIS installer 582 MiB, well under the 1.9 GB archive limit; the CUDA 13 runtime needs an NVIDIA driver that supports CUDA 13.
- **Plugins are removed; Prompts live in Chat.** The Plugins tab, its CLI commands and its API are gone, and prompt templates are now part of Chat. Per-thread system prompts, `/prompt` and template presets are not built yet. Fine-Tuning is hidden unless you turn on Settings > General > Appearance > Show experimental tabs.

### Also new in v0.10.0 (all platforms; check each against CHANGELOG.md before publishing)

- **Agent bridge.** An MCP endpoint (`/mcp`, off until you switch it on in Settings > Integrations > Agent access) lets an agent make images, video, speech, music and storyboards on the same queue as the Studios. An `AGENTS.md` guide is served with it.
- **Storyboard.** A film as a project folder of shots, takes and audio lanes, with a Board, a Plan tab (brief, proposed shots, brand kit, style references), draft and final passes, batches you can pause and resume across a restart, a Mixer with ducking, a rough-cut player, MP4 export at 16:9, 9:16 or 1:1, song-to-shots with cuts snapped to phrases, and a bundled four-shot sample film.
- **Chat.** Keep going for Studio chats (the app nudges the model and frees GPU memory while a render runs), a plan card with Draft, Standard and Final buttons and priced settings, waiting on a job for up to 30 minutes with a live progress line, Skills you import from a folder or zip (scanned and off by default), and message and tool-call times.
- **Chat models.** Recommended rows open first on a new machine with "Best fit", "Fastest", "Newest" and "Most popular" chips; mixture-of-experts models can keep their expert weights in system RAM ("Experts in system RAM" in the launch dialog, with a "Fits with offload" badge); the launch dialog shows one memory bar (in use now, model, cache, limit) for every engine; native llama.cpp can run its KV cache at q8_0 or q4_0.
- **Voice.** Voice Studio has Models and Library, a voice manager (filters, samples, use in Speak or Chat), delete for speech and dictation models, add-ons and the voice runtime, and any listed voice can read aloud in Chat or run a call. Every Speak result is saved to the Library.
- **Server and Settings.** Server > Activity shows a per-model timeline and event list (Logs folds into it); 110 % and 125 % UI scale; a stronger-borders accessibility switch; a clean-up panel in Settings > Disk; below 1180 px the sidebar becomes a top bar with a menu drawer.
- **Languages.** The nine shipping translations were filled on 2026-11-23 (5331 of 5344 strings each at the tip; the 13 newest, the memory bar and TensorFold launch notes, show in English until the last pass, FU-420); a Klingon (`tlh`) locale is registered as experimental and outside the coverage gate.

### Still hidden

- **MiniMax H3 video** stays off in this release. It is built and runs on the stable-diffusion.cpp lane, but its licence does not apply in the EU, the UK, South Korea or the US and a live check of its machine-generated tag is open, so no row is listed.

### Fixes worth knowing

- The speech add-on installs on Windows again (a new `kaldifst` release had no Windows wheel; it is pinned).
- The first model load no longer hangs for 300 seconds on Windows in the music and lip-sync worker processes.
- Killing the app from Task Manager no longer leaves `llama-server` running.
- FLUX on CUDA offloads to system memory only when the card cannot hold the model, a large speed-up on a 24 GB card.
- "Chat in a new thread" on a local model sends the model's own repo and opens a new thread.
- The text model library follows a moved Hugging Face cache folder, and a repo downloaded only for its drafter is no longer listed as a runnable model (FU-610, FU-611).
- A chat that names a model the app cannot load now says why instead of answering "Internal Server Error".
- Automatic and agent picks skip stable-diffusion.cpp rows when no `sd` binary is installed (FU-612), and the missing-binary message names the right build script for the platform.

### What was verified on Windows (one RTX 4090 24 GB, Windows 11, dev backend; all from `docs/release/cross-platform-test-tracker.md` and `docs/progress/rtx4090-progress.md`)

- **Full E2E suite, 2026-11-23, tip `8cc3a9b8`, llama.cpp b12651:** 23 phases pass, 1 skips (phase 216 needs MLX), 195 checks pass, 63 skip with a reason, 2 failed on the first run and both came from the test home, not the product (a missing b12651 CUDA build in the test home, and a cold-start `gpu-status` call over the CLI's 10 s limit); both pass on rerun. Phases include Chat on the CUDA llama-server (MTP, MoE expert offload, q8_0 KV), Image, Video, Voice, Agent bridge, Storyboard (a real 74 s draft) and Lip sync (a real 5.04 s clip; a two-take shot kept the better-scoring take).
- **Smoke run** 7 of 7. **Playwright** 250 passed, 17 failed: 3 fixed and rerun green, 14 are specs that mock Apple Silicon (FU-256) and fail on Windows by design.
- **Per feature, measured through the app:** Nunchaku FLUX.1 schnell INT4 (5.9 s warm at 1024 px; 4.8 s generation on 2026-11-23 from a freshly installed 1.2.1 wheel); the WSL2 vLLM bridge (load, chat, think split, GPU share and unload; Qwen3-0.6B at about 80 tokens per second); TensorFold 1.2.x engine (figures above); lip sync, music, speech and speech-to-text (figures above); the Windows installer with the CUDA `sd.exe` (582 MiB, a real Qwen-Image-2.1 Turbo image made by the packaged `sd.exe`: 8.0 s warm, 15.4 GiB peak). The installer was not installed or opened in this pass, and Storyboard and the agent bridge were exercised by E2E phases 9, 10, 16, 17, 20 and 22, not by a person (Keep going has unit and integration tests only).
- **Backend tests under WSL2 (Python 3.14, CPU torch):** 6628 passed, 0 failed, 80 skipped on `84315da2`; a rerun on `2d746618` exited 0. `check-upstreams.py` and `audit-dependencies.py` pass (no blocking advisory; 111 packages behind, 27 by a major version).
- **Not re-run on the final tip.** Seven product commits after the E2E run (`14793206` to `bb433c6d`: Keep going and Storyboard prompt fixes, the TensorFold launch dialog and the memory bar, the sd.cpp pick fix) have unit tests but no full E2E or Playwright run.

### Known limits (what was not tested)

- Everything above was run on one Windows 11 PC with one RTX 4090. Linux x86_64, ARM64 NVIDIA machines, RTX 50-series, cards below 24 GB and AMD or Intel GPUs have not been tried for these features. Native Linux is untested for all 19 open rows of the Linux section of the cross-platform tracker (WSL2 covered the backend tests, CUDA llama.cpp, TensorFold, vLLM, TriAttention and SageAttention only; AppImage, deb, Wayland and microphone are untested); Linux ARM64 stays "experimental, untested".
- **Apple Silicon was not run in this pass.** All 32 Mac rows of the tracker are still `todo`: the Python and frontend gates on the merged tree, MLX chat, dflash-mlx at the pin, mflux 0.24.0, ltx-2-mlx, Music on MLX, the packaged DMG, codesign and notarisation, and the microphone. TensorFold 1.3.4 family rows were not compared with the binary (FU-561).
- **Stable Audio 3 does not run on the Mac yet** (rows are CUDA-only; the owner decided it should, FU-600).
- The new features were run from the development tree, not from the installed app.
- Several downloads are gated by Hugging Face licences (Stable Audio 3, the FLUX.1 schnell base, FLUX 3 Image Klein, LTX); without an accepted licence the download fails with a message. FLUX 3 Image Klein and the FLUX.1 \[dev] Nunchaku pack are non-commercial or revenue-limited licences.
- Voices and music were checked by measurement and read-back, not by listening.
- On the Mac side, lip sync (`ltx-2-mlx a2v`) has never run. A sung lip-sync shot often has no usable take.
- A Mac user moving music weights between disks may be told about Windows Developer Mode (known wrong wording, FU-494).
- TensorFold on Windows needs a binary you build or host yourself; no release archive exists.
