## What's new in v0.10.0 (Windows and Linux with an NVIDIA GPU)

### Highlights

- **Music on Windows and Linux with an NVIDIA GPU.** ACE-Step 1.5 turbo and XL turbo write songs with lyrics and instrumentals from 10 to 600 seconds. On an RTX 4090 a 3-minute song takes about 3 seconds (turbo) or 4 seconds (XL) once the model is loaded; the 10-minute maximum takes 11 to 16 seconds. Turbo needs about 8 GB of video memory, XL 12 to 14 GB. Install the music pack from Music Models (about 6.6 GB, in its own folder). Linux, cards below 24 GB and other NVIDIA generations are untested.
- **Stable Audio 3** (sound effects and short music) joins Stable Audio Open on the same machines. Small SFX, Small Music and Medium are gated on Hugging Face: accept Stability's licence on each model page and add your token in Settings.
- **Speech-to-text on the GPU.** A new "More speech-to-text on the NVIDIA GPU" add-on (Voice > Models > Add-ons) adds Qwen3-ASR (1.7B and 0.6B), Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3, Nemotron 3.5 ASR and Granite Speech 5.0. On a 9-second clip the warm time is 0.15 to 0.2 seconds for Parakeet and 1 to 2.6 seconds for the others, and each read English and French back word for word apart from number formatting. A forced aligner gives word timings and speaker labels to the models that return none. The 30B Nemotron Transcribe row is listed but was not measured.
- **Natural voices on the GPU.** The speech add-on now carries Chatterbox (Turbo and Multilingual V3), VoxCPM2 and Orpheus, with voice design and cloning for the first three. Speed on an RTX 4090 is 5.7 seconds per 10 seconds of speech for Chatterbox Turbo, 12 for V3, 19 for VoxCPM2 (about twice real time, and 11 GB of system memory) and 7.8 for Orpheus. Cloned speech is watermarked as before. Nobody has listened to these yet; word-for-word read-back by a speech-to-text model is the only quality check so far.
- **New image and video models** (diffusers and stable-diffusion.cpp on CUDA): FLUX 3 Image Klein (7 s for 1024 px at 8 steps, plus a faster stable-diffusion.cpp Q8 row at 5.5 s), Qwen-Image-2.1 Turbo as a Q4 row (7.5 s), MOSS-Image SFT (53 s at 50 steps, 22 GB of video memory) and Kandinsky 6.0 Lite distilled video with a sound track (110 s for a 5-second clip at 864 by 480). Image editing with FLUX 3 Klein stays Mac-only for now. Krea 2 Turbo is not offered (gated, access not granted).
- **Lip sync (experimental, in progress).** Give Video Studio a vocal track and a start image and LTX-2.3 makes a clip where the mouth follows the audio, on an NVIDIA card with about 22 GB free. A 5-second 768 by 512 shot takes about 70 seconds warm (157 seconds the first time). Sync is better than chance in our measurement (correlation 0.27 to 0.50 against 0.16 to 0.40 for chance) but a person has not judged it. Singing works only partly, and the Apple Silicon path has never run. Treat it as a preview.
- **Chat models.** New GGUF rows with measured 4090 speeds: Gemma 4 12B QAT (93 tok/s, 115 to 120 with its drafter), Gemma 4 26B-A4B QAT (160 tok/s, about 175 with its drafter), Nemotron 3.5 Lightning Q4_0 (232 tok/s) and Qwen3.6 35B-A3B UD-IQ4_XS with MTP heads (170 tok/s, no real gain from its heads). Speculative decoding stays off by default. Larger quants of the same families are listed with their sizes and the fit check; they were not run.
- **Nunchaku (SVDQuant INT4) FLUX.1** on NVIDIA: FLUX.1 schnell at 1024 px in about 6 seconds warm. Nunchaku is not on PyPI under that name, so you install its wheel yourself. The Dev INT4 pack carries the FLUX.1 \[dev] non-commercial licence.
- **vLLM in WSL2.** "Install vLLM in WSL" in Settings > Accelerators installs vLLM inside your Ubuntu distro, and the app talks to it as an engine. A 0.6B model answers at about 80 tokens per second through the bridge and gives its memory back on unload. It needs WSL2 and an NVIDIA driver, and the installer names the Ubuntu packages it needs. Only a 24 GB card and one small model were tried.
- **Windows CUDA llama.cpp and stable-diffusion.cpp.** Settings > Accelerators installs the CUDA llama-server (b12441), and stable-diffusion.cpp builds with CUDA on Windows; the installer carries NVIDIA's CUDA runtime DLLs for it under NVIDIA's licence (see THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md).
- **Plugins are removed; Prompts live in Chat.** The Plugins tab, its CLI commands and its API are gone, and prompt templates are now part of Chat. Per-thread system prompts, `/prompt` and template presets are not built yet.

### Still hidden

- **MiniMax H3 video** stays off in this release. It is built and runs on the stable-diffusion.cpp lane, but its licence does not apply in the EU, the UK, South Korea or the US and a live check of its machine-generated tag is open, so no row is listed.

### Fixes worth knowing

- The speech add-on installs on Windows again (a new `kaldifst` release had no Windows wheel; it is pinned).
- The first model load no longer hangs for 300 seconds on Windows in the music and lip-sync worker processes.
- Killing the app from Task Manager no longer leaves `llama-server` running.
- FLUX on CUDA offloads to system memory only when the card cannot hold the model, a large speed-up on a 24 GB card.
- "Chat in a new thread" on a local model sends the model's own repo and opens a new thread.

### Known limits (what was not tested)

- Everything above was run on one Windows 11 PC with one RTX 4090. Linux x86_64, ARM64 NVIDIA machines, RTX 50-series, cards below 24 GB and AMD or Intel GPUs have not been tried for these features.
- The new features were run from the development tree, not from the installed app.
- Several downloads are gated by Hugging Face licences (Stable Audio 3, the FLUX.1 schnell base, LTX); without an accepted licence the download fails with a message.
- Voices and music were checked by measurement and read-back, not by listening.
- On the Mac side, lip sync (`ltx-2-mlx a2v`) has never run.
